# Crypto PMM Frameworks & Playbooks

## 1. The Crypto Positioning Canvas

Traditional positioning frameworks (April Dunford's "Obviously Awesome") need adaptation for crypto. Here's the modified canvas:

### Elements

**Category:** What type of protocol/product is this? (Don't force novel categories — most projects fit existing ones with a twist)

**Target Users (Layered):**
* Primary: Who uses the protocol directly? (developers, traders, LPs, stakers, node operators)
* Secondary: Who benefits indirectly? (dApp end users, DAO members, ecosystem projects)
* Narrative audience: Who needs to believe in the project even if they don't use it? (CT, VCs, researchers)

**Competitive Alternatives:** What does the user do today if your protocol doesn't exist? This is often NOT another protocol — it's a manual process, a centralized service, or doing nothing.

**Unique Value (Protocol-Specific):**
* Technical differentiator (architecture, security model, performance)
* Economic differentiator (fee structure, token incentives, yield)
* Ecosystem differentiator (composability, existing integrations, developer tooling)
* Trust differentiator (team reputation, audit history, track record, decentralization level)

**Proof Points:** On-chain metrics > marketing claims. TVL, daily active addresses, transaction volume, developer activity (GitHub commits, new repos), protocol revenue, bridge volume.

### Positioning Tiers

Not all audiences need the same positioning:

| Audience | Positioning Focus | Channel |
|----------|------------------|---------|
| Developers | Technical architecture, DX, docs quality | GitHub, Discord, DevRel events |
| DeFi power users | Yield, gas efficiency, composability | CT, Dune dashboards, DeFi aggregators |
| Institutional | Security, compliance, reliability | Direct sales, conferences, reports |
| Ecosystem/BD | Integration ease, co-marketing, grants | Partnerships, governance forums |
| Retail/casual | Simple value prop, brand, social proof | CT, TikTok, influencer campaigns |

## 2. Crypto GTM Strategy Framework

### Phase 0: Pre-Launch (3-6 months before)
* Testnet with incentivized participation
* Developer documentation and early SDK access
* Seed community formation (Discord, early contributors)
* Research partnerships and academic validation
* Security audits (multiple, publicized)
* Ambassador/early contributor programs

### Phase 1: Launch (Weeks -2 to +4)
* Mainnet/protocol launch event
* Announcement sequencing (don't blow your load day one — stagger reveals)
* KOL seeding and briefings (NOT paid shills — informed voices)
* Launch metrics dashboard (transparent, real-time)
* Initial integrations go live
* AMA/Spaces circuit
* Press and media (The Block, CoinDesk, Blockworks — but CT matters more)

### Phase 2: Growth (Months 1-6)
* Incentive programs (points, airdrops, grants) — designed with clear objectives and sunset plans
* Developer ecosystem expansion (hackathons, bounties, grants)
* Protocol partnerships and integrations
* Content engine (threads, research, docs, case studies)
* Community-driven initiatives (governance, ambassadors, regional chapters)
* Exchange listing strategy (if applicable)

### Phase 3: Maturity (6+ months)
* Protocol governance marketing (making governance accessible and engaging)
* Ecosystem storytelling (highlight what's being built on top)
* Institutional onboarding
* Competitive response and repositioning
* V2/V3 migration communications

### GTM Anti-Patterns in Crypto
* Launching a token before product-market fit
* Over-relying on paid KOL campaigns without organic community
* Copying another protocol's incentive program without understanding why it worked for them
* Treating Discord member count as a success metric
* Ignoring governance communications until there's a crisis
* Running a points program without a clear conversion strategy
* Announcing partnerships that are just "we deployed on their chain"

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy for Crypto Protocols

### Level 1: Core Narrative (The "Why")
One sentence that captures why this protocol needs to exist. This should resonate emotionally and intellectually.

Examples:
* Ethereum: "A world computer that anyone can program"
* Uniswap: "Trade anything, anywhere, anytime"
* ENS: "Your identity across the decentralized web"

### Level 2: Value Propositions (The "What")
3-4 concrete value props that map to user needs. Each should be provable.

Format: [Benefit] + [Mechanism] + [Proof]
* "10x lower gas fees through [technical approach], proven by [on-chain data]"
* "Composable across 12 chains via [architecture], integrated by [notable protocols]"

### Level 3: Feature Messaging (The "How")
Technical details, specs, and capabilities. This layer exists for developers, researchers, and technical evaluators.

### Level 4: Social/Community Messaging
How the community talks about the protocol. Memes, shorthand, internal language. You don't control this — you seed it and let it evolve.

### Messaging Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
* Lead with what the user can DO, not what the technology IS
* Use concrete numbers (throughput, fees, TVL) over vague claims
* Acknowledge tradeoffs honestly — crypto audiences respect transparency
* Let the tech speak through documentation and live demos
* Create messaging that community members can easily remix and reshare

**Don't:**
* Claim to be "the fastest/cheapest/most secure" without rigorous proof
* Use enterprise marketing jargon ("synergy", "next-generation", "revolutionary")
* Ignore your competitors — acknowledge them and articulate your differentiation clearly
* Over-promise on decentralization timelines
* Use fear-based messaging about competitors (it backfires in crypto culture)

## 4. Crypto Content Strategy Matrix

### Content Types by Funnel Stage

**Awareness (Top of Funnel):**
* Twitter/X threads explaining the problem you solve
* Podcast/Spaces appearances
* Memes and cultural content
* Short-form video explainers
* Research reports and market analysis

**Consideration (Mid Funnel):**
* Technical deep dives and architecture explainers
* Comparison guides (honest, not hit pieces)
* Case studies from early users/integrators
* AMAs and live demos
* Dune dashboards and transparent metrics

**Conversion (Bottom Funnel):**
* Documentation and integration guides
* Video tutorials and walkthroughs
* Migration guides (from competitor or v1)
* Onboarding campaigns with incentives
* Direct developer outreach and support

**Retention/Expansion:**
* Governance proposals and updates
* Ecosystem highlight content
* Community spotlights
* Roadmap updates and transparency reports
* Protocol revenue and growth dashboards

## 5. Competitive Analysis Framework for Crypto

### The 5-Layer Competitive Stack

**Layer 1: Technical Architecture**
* Consensus mechanism / execution environment
* Security model and audit history
* Performance benchmarks (real, not theoretical)
* Upgrade path and technical roadmap

**Layer 2: Economic Design**
* Token utility and value accrual
* Fee structure and revenue model
* Incentive mechanisms (staking, LP rewards, points)
* Token distribution and vesting schedule

**Layer 3: Ecosystem & Composability**
* Number and quality of integrations
* Developer activity and tooling
* DeFi TVL and application diversity
* Cross-chain presence

**Layer 4: Community & Governance**
* Community size AND engagement quality
* Governance participation rates
* Developer relations and support quality
* Brand sentiment and narrative strength

**Layer 5: Go-to-Market Execution**
* Launch cadence and quality
* Content and thought leadership output
* Partnership strategy and execution
* Marketing spend efficiency

### Competitive Response Playbook

**When a competitor launches a similar feature:**
1. Don't panic-tweet. Assess the actual technical delta.
2. If your implementation is better: create a clear comparison (technical, not snarky)
3. If theirs is comparable: focus on ecosystem, track record, and roadmap
4. If theirs is better: acknowledge it, communicate your own roadmap honestly
5. Always: let your community defend you (they will if you've built trust)

**When a competitor copies your positioning:**
1. Go deeper. They can copy the tagline but not the substance.
2. Double down on proof points and technical content
3. Create content that demonstrates depth they can't replicate
4. Build relationships with key voices who understand the nuance

## 6. Token Launch & TGE Marketing Framework

### Pre-TGE Communications
* Build anticipation without making promises that could be construed as securities marketing
* Focus on utility and ecosystem value, never price appreciation
* Ensure legal review of all token-related communications
* Create clear documentation of token utility, distribution, and governance role

### TGE Execution
* Coordinate announcement timing with exchange listings
* Prepare community for initial volatility (set expectations)
* Have real-time support ready for claiming/distribution issues
* Monitor and respond to community sentiment actively

### Post-TGE Narrative Management
* Shift narrative from "launch" to "utility and adoption"
* Highlight real usage metrics, not price
* Communicate unlock schedules transparently
* Use governance as a narrative tool — give token holders real power and communicate it

### Regulatory Considerations for Marketing
* Never make price predictions or imply investment returns
* Focus on utility and functionality in all public communications
* "Decentralization theater" is risky — be honest about your decentralization roadmap
* Different jurisdictions have different rules — don't assume US-only compliance is sufficient
* Work with legal counsel on all token marketing materials

## 7. Developer Marketing in Crypto

### The Developer Funnel

**Discovery:** Developer learns your protocol exists
* Hackathon presence, grants program visibility
* Developer-focused content (not marketing fluff — real technical content)
* Open source contributions and GitHub activity
* Word of mouth from other developers

**Evaluation:** Developer assesses whether to build on your platform
* Documentation quality (this IS your conversion rate)
* SDK/API design and developer experience
* Testnet accessibility and faucet reliability
* Example projects and starter templates
* Support responsiveness (Discord, GitHub issues)

**Adoption:** Developer starts building
* Onboarding flow (time to first transaction)
* Migration guides from competing platforms
* Technical support quality
* Grant/funding opportunities

**Advocacy:** Developer becomes an evangelist
* Spotlight their project in your channels
* Include them in governance discussions
* Feature their feedback in roadmap decisions
* Create opportunities for them to present/teach

### DevRel Anti-Patterns
* Hiring DevRel who can't code
* Writing docs that are technically correct but unusable
* Running hackathons without follow-up support for winners
* Treating developer community as a marketing channel rather than a product feedback loop
* Ignoring documentation bugs/gaps reported by developers

## 8. Airdrop & Incentive Program Strategy

### Design Principles
* **Clear objective:** What behavior are you incentivizing? (TVL, transactions, governance participation, bridging)
* **Sybil resistance:** How do you prevent gaming? (on-chain history, identity verification, activity thresholds)
* **Sunset plan:** When and how does the program end? What sustainable mechanism replaces it?
* **Value alignment:** Does the incentive attract users who will stay, or mercenary capital that will leave?

### Airdrop Playbook

**Points Programs:**
* Pros: Predictable behavior, measurable engagement, builds anticipation
* Cons: Attract farmers, create entitlement, can feel extractive
* Best for: Protocols with clear usage patterns to incentivize
* Key: Be transparent about conversion mechanics. Opacity breeds resentment.

**Retroactive Airdrops:**
* Pros: Reward genuine users, generate goodwill, surprise factor
* Cons: Hard to execute fairly, always controversy about criteria
* Best for: Rewarding early adopters of a protocol that already has organic usage
* Key: Over-communicate your criteria rationale. Accept that some people will be unhappy.

**Quest/Campaign-Based:**
* Pros: Educate users, drive specific actions, measurable
* Cons: Can feel like work, quality of engagement varies
* Best for: Onboarding new users to a complex protocol
* Key: Make the quests actually teach something useful, not just busywork.

### Incentive Program Metrics
* Retention rate after incentive ends (the REAL measure)
* Cost per retained user vs. cost per acquired user
* Percentage of incentivized actions that become organic habits
* Community sentiment before, during, and after the program
* Impact on protocol revenue and sustainable metrics

## 9. Crisis Communications in Crypto

### Exploit/Hack Response
1. Acknowledge immediately. Even "We are aware of an issue and investigating" is better than silence.
2. Provide technical details as you have them. The community includes security researchers — they will find out anyway.
3. Communicate user impact clearly: Is fund at risk? What should users do RIGHT NOW?
4. Post-mortem within 48-72 hours. Be thorough and honest.
5. Remediation plan with timeline.

### Governance Crisis Response
1. Don't take sides publicly if you're the protocol team.
2. Provide facts and analysis, not spin.
3. Facilitate discussion rather than controlling it.
4. If the team has a position, state it clearly and let governance proceed.

### Market/Token Crisis
1. Don't comment on price. Ever.
2. Focus on fundamentals: development progress, usage metrics, roadmap execution.
3. Increase transparency and communication cadence.
4. Highlight long-term vision and near-term deliverables.

### General Crisis Principles
* Speed > perfection in initial communication
* Transparency builds trust faster than spin
* Your community will forgive mistakes but not cover-ups
* Document everything for the post-mortem
* Have a crisis playbook BEFORE you need one
